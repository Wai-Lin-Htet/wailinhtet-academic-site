The pattern changed in 2023. In the old 100-mark papers (2021–22) Chapter 6 was 41.9% of the paper. Since 2023 (80 marks, 1h 30m) it is about 20%, and the paper spreads out: Chapters 1, 8, 10 and 7 all carry real weight. Revise from 2023–2026 papers first — 2021–22 papers are extra scenario practice, not the model.

2. The topics that appear almost every time

  • Security: threats & protection — 433 marks, in 34 of 36 papers. Passwords and 2FA, phishing / pharming / smishing, encryption, firewalls, biometrics, eSafety.
  • Communication applications — 417 marks, in 32 of 36 papers. Email, audio/video and web conferencing, VoIP, web layers and file formats.
  • Networks & devices — 263 marks (28 of 36 papers). Input & output devices — 238 (28 of 36). Systems life cycle — 200 (23 of 36).
  • Health issues (RSI, eyestrain, back problems) — small marks (6–8) but asked in 20 of 36 papers. Students write one line and stop; write the full answer.
  • Validation checks — 155 marks across 21 papers. The exam wants the named check (range, length, presence, format, type) matched to the field, not a generic definition.

3. CLI vs GUI — the exact answer

  • CLI was asked only 3 times in 6 years — 14 marks total (June 2024 twice, March 2026 once).
  • GUI was asked 5 times — 28 marks.
  • The whole user-interface family (CLI, GUI, dialogue-based, gesture-based) is 12 questions, 64 marks, in 11 of 36 papers. The exam rotates the pair: CLI vs GUI in 2024, CLI vs dialogue-based in March 2026. Learn all four types and how to compare any two — revising only “CLI vs GUI” misses the 2026 question.

4. The 2026 paper (March 2026, the newest one)

Ch 1 — 17 marks, Ch 10 — 15, Ch 2 — 13, Ch 7 — 10. Biggest single question: Q5, Sensors for data collection, 13 marks. Ch 3 and Ch 9 had zero marks.

One paper is a hint, not a rule — but Chapter 1 + Chapter 10 + sensors + validation is where the newest marks sit.

5. Five A* tips

  1. This is an extended-answer paper. 296 of 481 questions are worth 6 marks or more, and they carry 80.3% of all marks. Only 51 questions are 1–2 marks. Drill the 6–8 mark “describe / explain / discuss” answer: a point, an expansion, and an example tied to the scenario.
  2. Practise the big scenario question. 107 questions are worth 9+ marks (40.2% of all marks). They bundle 3–5 sub-topics under one scenario — the A* skill is switching topics inside one question.
  3. Master five blocks and you hold ~70% of the paper: (a) ICT Applications scenarios — spreadsheets, databases, expert systems, control/measurement; (b) Security; (c) Chapter 1 fundamentals — hardware vs software, RAM vs ROM, SSD vs HDD, all four user interfaces; (d) Communication; (e) Systems life cycle + validation.
  4. Don’t over-revise the old syllabus. Spreadsheets, databases and document production were ~40% of the 2021–22 papers and a small slice since 2023.
  5. Cheap marks, don’t skip them: Audience / house style is only ~2% of marks, but the questions are almost free — one 2023 question on audience and house style alone was worth 13 marks.
